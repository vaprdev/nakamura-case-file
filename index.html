FIELD NOTES — DET. xxxxxxxxI.

The file came to me the way bad news always does: in a manila folder, late on a Friday, from a man who wouldn't look me in the eye.

It was thin for a life. Fourteen items, three of them missing. A woman boiled down to paper, and the paper already going yellow at the edges.

Barbara K. Nakamura carried the mail for eleven hundred people who never learned her name. Every memo, every rumor, every midnight apology from an executive to someone who wasn't his wife passed through her hands, and she never dropped a single one.

On the third of November she didn't come in. The building didn't blink. Buildings like that never do. They just keep humming, lights on, air breathing through the vents, pretending there was never anybody there to miss.

I lit a cigarette and opened the file. I should have left it closed.
